CASIO STUDIO REFINEMENT V2

Starting point: the previously delivered scene-match/casio_f91w_scene_match.blend.
This is an incremental lighting/camera/floor pass, not a rebuild. The previous
scene, render, script and validation are retained in source-snapshot/.

Changes directed by the reference comparison
1. Framing: 100 mm level front camera retained. Projected manufactured bounds
   increased from 62% to 65.5% frame height (+5.65% relative size); framing raised
   so the bottom is at 70.7%, horizontal center 50%. Bounds include some empty
   corners around curved geometry; visible silhouette is slightly smaller.
2. Key: emitter width reduced from 6 mm to 3 mm, length from 100 mm to 75 mm,
   spread from 0.22 to 0.11 rad. Position/aim moved upward and toward the watch
   center. Power reduced to 0.0055 W to compensate for concentration. This
   produces a slimmer central path instead of the previous broad strap sheen.
3. Separation: both side strips moved forward to y=-25 mm and aimed at the
   case, with restrained power increases. The front fill was brought closer
   and kept low. The case edges and buttons are clearer without lifting the
   entire background or lower strap.
4. Reflection panel: narrowed and shortened; luminance reduced. Camera-ray
   visibility remains disabled, reflection contribution retained.
5. Floor: grazing emitter lowered, reduced in height and power, then shortened
   for softer falloff. The reflective-to-matte foreground transition was
   extended to retain a faint, gradually fading strap reflection.
6. Red accent: power reduced 70%, emitter narrowed slightly. It remains confined
   to the underside rather than becoming a visible red spotlight.

All manufactured meshes, materials, editable printing, LCD layers and their
transforms were preserved. No material tuning proved necessary. Neutral AgX,
exposure -0.65, dark world, physical floor and 16-bit PNG output are retained.

Preview validation
Three 804 x 962 previews addressed the named defects: initial composition/key/
case separation, floor brightness, then floor falloff and reflection length.
Equal-size reference/previous/refined comparison was inspected. The revised
key is narrower, case edges separate more clearly, and the lower strap remains
subdued. At 402 x 481 display size, side-margin luminance profiles place the
floor peak near 70.5% height versus reference 70.3% and previous 72.1%. The
bright floor core is approximately 6 pixels wide at half maximum versus 13 in
the previous image. These are display-image measurements, not physical data.

Remaining differences
The reference's wider metal chronograph case, shorter-looking strap sections
and directional brushed highlights differ from this preserved resin Casio.
The Casio LCD and colored printing also respond differently from an analog
black dial. Its silhouette cannot be made identical without changing the
product. Floor and reflection falloff are closer but not a pixel-identical
reconstruction. The 402 x 481 source limits inference about the original rig.

Execution used one agent and isolated Blender processes orchestrated through
verified MCP (Blender 5.2.3 LTS, add-on 1.8, protocol 15). No new research,
geometry rebuilding or product shader rewriting was performed.

Final validation
The delivered hero, three-way comparison and native face/strap/floor crops were
actually inspected. LCD is clean at native resolution, the case grain remains
visible, and the narrow overhead band stays restrained. Floor reflection is
faint and smoothly fades; the red underside is barely visible.
Reopened-project validation passed: manufactured geometry and product shader
fingerprints unchanged, case bounds 35.2 x 38.2 x 8.5 mm, packed fonts retained.
Original source hashes are unchanged and the live Blender scene remains its
original three objects.

Final output: 2400 x 2872, 1536 Cycles samples, adaptive threshold 0.0007,
denoising enabled, 16-bit PNG. Final render time: 876.28 seconds (14m 36s).
Only one full-quality render was performed in this pass; three small previews
preceded it. Render time is separate from token usage; no token-savings claim.
