## Template for the next Nautilus variants (5711/1A row 107, 5712/1A row 109: 350 px JPEG photos with a white background, no alpha)

Files already in place (untracked): `references/patek-nautilus-5711-1a/patek-57111a_original.jpg` (350 x 492) and `references/patek-nautilus-5712-1a/patek-57121a_original.jpg` (350 x 480). Both are 40 mm steel Nautilus, so `case_mm` = 40. 5712/1A has extra dial elements (moon phase, power-reserve and date sub-dials at 6 / 8 / 4:30 with their own small hands): those stay in the dial map as photographed; add the extra needles as `hands` entries only if they show clearly (the step takes the three hands of this model; a fourth needle needs a code change: copy `watch_patek_nautilus41.py`). **Template (keep as is):** `pipeline/steps/watch_patek_nautilus41.py` and `pipeline/patek_nautilus41_prep.py` (read-only unless a code change is needed), the kit lighting, materials, pose, heights (`case` body_z 6.6, bezel_z 8.9, chamfer_z 8.2, band dz 0.45, dial z 7.0, hands z, crown z 5.0, bracelet z0 / z_side / lift / piece_lift / bevels / gap 0.05), `rays` 360 and `aperture.n` 360 (they must match), hands emission 0.43, steel emission 0.285 and dial 0.31 / bracelet 0.275 / 0.29 as a start, the structure of the measure file (pivot, anchor rows, bezel_outer, aperture, hands, hub, crowns, bracelet rows / pieces), the comparison conventions (kit frame 1280 square at half of the WORKING scale, pivot at the centre; the working scale is trace.scale_px_per_mm).

**Per photo (measure, never trust the 5811 numbers):**
1. `pivot` (hub screw centre, 6x crop with a 10 px grid), `anchor.rows` (rows of the straight left flank at the ears) and `case_mm` 40: the prep derives the scale from the pivot-to-left-flank distance = case_mm / 2, so the pivot x must be right to a working px (the 5811 case is 41 mm; a 5711 flank at the ears is the same shape, 40 mm).
2. `crown_replace` rows and x (the notch line = right flank) and the crown `base_px` / `profile_px` (radius, axial mm from the crown base; axis y can sit below the pivot).
3. `bezel_outer` (centre, half axes, exponent: read 5 points of the outer edge of the polished band; the Nautilus gives about 0.99 x 0.99 of the pivot-to-flank distance and exponent about 2.3 to 2.4) and `bevel_px` (the band width, about 1.2 mm); `aperture` thresholds if the window scan fails (it asserts) and `chamfer_px`.
4. `hands` (angles from rows rotated about the pivot with gradient edge fits, lengths, tail) and `hub_r_px`; then the spec `hands.profile` / `lume` lists: frame half widths, lume inlay half widths, stem flare, round tips, seconds tail (these are in the spec as [s, half width] mm lists).
5. `bracelet.rows_px` (outer-link rows: y start / end per side; read the dark joint lines at an outer column, expect 3 rows above and 3 to 4 below) and `bracelet.pieces_px` (polished centre pieces: y0 y1 x0 x1 and corner radius r: dark-outline scans along rows through each piece). The case slab tips `case.tip_top` / `tip_bottom` in the spec are the end-link joints (multiples of 0.25 mm whose sum is a multiple of 0.25 and which overlap the first rows by 0.05 mm: tip = joint row distance rounded up to 0.25).
6. Emissions: dial plate (blue 0.31 here; the steel 5711/1A blue is a darker gradient, check both ways), steel, bracelet top / bottom, hands, so the region means are within a few percent (`python3 -B -m pipeline.zoom_check <baseline dir>` and a region script like the one used here).

**What has to change for a 350 px JPEG on white (no alpha, about 6.5 px/mm):**
- `measure.upscale`: 4 (Lanczos, 350 -> 1400 px wide, about 26 px/mm working); every px value in the measure file is then in WORKING px (4 x source px) and the prep records the factor in the trace. Enlargement does not recover detail: print, hands, indices, lume lines will be soft at zoom (a 3x crop equals 12x of the source); say so in the Zoom check. The step needs no change (it reads the map size and scale from the json).
- `measure.mask`: {"bg_tol": 12} builds the silhouette from a flood fill (fixed range, so the soft edge cannot creep into the watch) of the near-white background from the border, 3x3 opening, 0.8 px Gaussian soft edge. Check the mask: tolerate more (20) if a drop shadow is dark at the bracelet end, keep it small if the polished band is near white (a white band touching the white background is read as background: check the bezel rim and the ear tips; if it leaks, paint those rows by hand in a scratch copy of the photo, record it). On a quick test (this photo shrunk to 350 px, composited on white, JPEG q88, x4) the mask matched the real alpha at IoU 0.96 (tolerance 12) to 0.97 (20); the residue was the photo's drop shadow. The silhouette edge rows are NOT as sharp as the PNG (about 2 working px of blur): measure the flank rows on the thresholded mask, not on edge gradients.
- JPEG artifacts: dial map and steel map are built from the enlarged image; use `steel_quality` 90 and `steel_max_px` 2048 as here (the enlarged watch is about 1.4k x 2.3k, the cap may not bite). Dial map `quality` is 93.
- The window scan (`aperture`) needs `min_run_px` / `r_start_px` / `r_end_px` in WORKING px (scale them by the upscale factor over the 5811 values: min_run_px about 25 x scale / 33.56 ...); fit them so no ray fails.
- Gradient fits on the hands are noisier on a 4x enlarged JPEG: median over 20 rows, accept angles within 0.3 deg.
- Kit preset: the half-working-scale convention puts the frame at working_scale / 2 px per mm; for a 26 px/mm working scale that is 13 px/mm and the 1280 frame holds 98 mm (more than enough); copy the preset, set `scale_px_per_mm` and keep `center_px` 640 / 640; the compared photo is the working image (enlarged) downscaled by 0.5.
