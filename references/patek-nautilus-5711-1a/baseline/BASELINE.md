# Baseline: PATEK PHILIPPE Nautilus 5711/1A (saved 2026-11-07, awaiting designer approval)

Front-relief build on the 40 mm steel / blue embossed dial / steel bracelet catalogue photo, which is only a 350 x 492 px JPEG (white background, no alpha; the host serves no larger version). FOLLOWER of the Nautilus template built for the 5811/1G (template step and kit lighting reused, own prep copy, measure, spec, job, kit scale). One build, then one emission pass (dial 0.31 -> 0.315 -> 0.325 -> 0.34). Approval is the designer's; this file is not a similarity score.

**Honest note on the source.** The photo is about 6.6 px/mm (the 5811/1G photo is 33.6 px/mm, five times more). Geometry, joints and the silhouette were measured on a x4 Lanczos enlargement to about +-1 source px (+-0.15 mm at 40 mm); the dial print, hands, indices, minute dots and brushed grain are reproduced only at the photo's own resolution (enlarging does not recover detail, no lettering, grain or geometry was invented), so this GLB is soft at zoom compared with the 5811/1G.

Source handling (recorded): the original stays untouched. The prep enlarges it x4 (Lanczos, 1400 x 1968, 26.5064 working px/mm) and builds the silhouette mask by a flood fill of the near-white background from the border (fixed range, tolerance 12, 3x3 opening, 0.8 px Gaussian soft edge). The flood fill reads the soft drop shadow under the bracelet as foreground, so `measure.mask.clip` removes it: below working row 1700 everything left / right of two hand-measured bracelet edge lines (x 430 / 449 / 463 left, 951 / 926 / 908 right at rows 1700 / 1800 / 1880) and below row 1882 is background (checked by contour overlay on the case, the bracelet sides and ends; the white polished bezel band did not leak). The scale is anchored on both flanks (left flank x 166.x, right flank x about 1225, 40 mm -> 26.5064 px/mm; the pivot is 7 px right of the case centre, so the template rule "pivot to left flank = case_mm / 2" would be 1.5 % off).

Case outline = the flood-fill silhouette (ears included) between the end-link tips (+20.25 / -21.75 mm), crown rows 798..930 replaced by the right flank line. Known differences: the background and the soft drop shadow below the bracelet are not part of the asset; the embossed lines, applied indices, print, minute dots, date window, brushed / polished facets, crown flutes and the bracelet's brushed grain are texture only and as soft as the 350 px photo; the 3D hands, crown, bezel band / chamfer step, dial plane, bracelet rows and polished pieces are geometry; the joints between rows are the photo's dark lines plus a 0.05 mm gap showing the projected backing; the case slab end links slope toward the bracelet by a straight ramp (real shape curves); polished centre pieces are flat-topped rounded rectangles (the photo's domed highlights are texture); the crown is a lathe with a mean radius profile (the photo's crown is slightly tilted: its upper half reaches a larger radius than the lower); hour / minute / seconds centre lines sit up to 1 px (working) off the pivot in the photo, not modelled; the bezel outer superellipse follows the polished band within about 3 working px; heights have no side view, back flat. Textures: the steel map is 3x the source (836 x 1400) and the dial map 2.7x (720 px): more would only store the Lanczos blur.

## Zoom check

`zoom_check.jpg` (3x crops, reference | render): upper dial, hands and hub, lower dial. What matches: the PATEK PHILIPPE print, the applied indices with their lume inlays, the double baton at 12, the hour / minute hands (edges, lume line and round tips line up with the photo within about 1 px of the half-size frame), the hub discs, the seconds hand shaft and counterweight, the minute dots and the bezel edge. What is soft and why: everything fine (GENEVE, SWISS print, the embossing gradient, the bezel's brushed grain) is exactly as soft as the 350 px photo at a 3x zoom (equal to 12x of the source pixels: lettering edges are blurred, JPEG block noise from the source is visible in the print); the brushed grain on the bezel face is a little smoother in the render than in the photo (steel map at 3x the source). The seconds shaft is a touch thicker than the photo's hair line at zoom (the source blurs a 0.2 mm line). No halos, dark gaps, stair-steps or misplaced hand edges were visible in the crops.

**Template (keep):** kit lighting, materials, heights, bevels, bracelet gap, `rays` 360 = `aperture.n` 360, hand z, hands emission 0.43, steel 0.285, bracelet 0.275 / 0.29, the measure-file structure, the comparison frame convention (1280 square at half the working scale, pivot at the centre; working image = source x4 downscaled by 0.5), the x4 Lanczos + flood-fill mask approach.

**Per photo (measure, never reuse the 5711 numbers):** `pivot` (hub centre, 6x crop with a 10 px grid on the x4 working image; `python3 -B crop` style grids as used here), `anchor.rows` (left flank rows at the ears) and `anchor.right_rows` (right flank rows above and below the crown; the scale is then width / 40), `crown_replace` rows (the crown's visible extent), crown `base_px` / `profile_px`, `bezel_outer` (read the polished band on the pivot row and column: outer edges give centre and half axes; band width = bevel_px; exponent 2.35 held), `aperture` (r_start must lie beyond the index batons, min_run about 40; the scan asserts), hands (angles from a PCA of the red-channel bright pixels, tips and widths from rotated strips; the 5711 spec profiles are the starting point), `hub_r_px`, bracelet `rows_px` (joint rows from dark-line scans on an outer-link column) and `pieces_px` (dark-outline scans along rows and columns), the `mask.clip` lines for the drop shadow (the flood fill leaks into the shadow below the bracelet: measure the bracelet edge from the dark outline on rows with `gray < 192` from outside in), the spec `tip_top` / `tip_bottom` (joint distance rounded up to 0.25, sum a multiple of 0.25, first row overlaps by about 0.05 mm) and `verified_mm.diameter` 40, then emissions (dial 0.34 here for this blue gradient dial: tune the plate mean to within 1 %; the response is slow, about 0.4 grey level per 0.015).
